# Governance map prototype

The governance map is a reviewed teaching example in the California statewide voter guide. It answers what a ballot choice controls before asking the reader to open each relationship. The first examples cover the Governor and Proposition 4 on the November 2026 ballot. The proposition removes a legal prohibition; it does not itself create a public financing program.

## Source and editorial contract

[`governance-map.ts`](../apps/expo/src/utils/governance-map.ts) holds fixed example data: a short takeaway, a visible limit, relationship nodes, and a source ID for every relationship. Source IDs resolve to official records linked on the screen. These statements are editorial paraphrases of formal authority, not candidate statements or generated predictions. The UI must keep campaign promises, future policy choices, and election outcomes outside the formal-power path. A conditional branch says what a Yes or No result _would_ permit if the measure passes or fails; it must not imply one reader's vote decides the result.

The office example uses the [California Constitution](https://clerk.assembly.ca.gov/sites/clerk.assembly.ca.gov/files/2023-24_Constitution_Final_wCover.pdf), especially Article V, Section 2 for the January 4, 2027 term start, the [Department of Finance budget process](https://dof.ca.gov/budget/budget-process-overview/), the [Governor's appointments page](https://www.gov.ca.gov/join-the-administration/government-appointments/), [Government Code §1774](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1774.&lawCode=GOV) for Senate confirmation, and the [California courts' description of judicial independence](https://courts.ca.gov/goal-ii-independence-and-accountability). The courts path represents review when a legal dispute is brought, not an automatic review of every Governor action. Both examples use the [Secretary of State results and canvass explanation](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/voter-info/election-results.htm) for the interval between casting ballots and a certified statewide result. The measure example also uses the [Legislative Analyst's Office analysis](https://lao.ca.gov/BallotAnalysis/Proposition?number=4&year=2026) and the [Secretary of State guide](https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/4/index.htm). Proposition numbers and the Secretary of State page URL can be reused in later elections, so measure matching requires the exact state, election date, and number; the URL alone is insufficient.

Before changing a relationship, an editor should reopen its official source, compare the exact authority and exceptions, verify the election date, and review the on-screen summary and text version together. A relationship without adequate evidence should be removed. The app does not synthesize map nodes from provider summaries.

## Entry points and reusable pieces

The [map route](../apps/expo/src/app/governance-map.tsx) presents a guided three-stage scroll through [one shared illustrated scene vocabulary](../apps/expo/src/components/GovernanceScene.tsx): voters and a ballot box, a certified result, then changed authority and institutional checks. The fixed bottom stage control jumps to each location; ordinary scrolling updates its highlighted stage. The proposition illustration shows the existing ban beside the count, then separate Yes and No gates. The No path stops with the ban in place. Only the Yes path reaches state and local officials, whose later decision can either create a qualifying program or decline to do so. The office illustration connects the Governor's budget, bill, and appointment powers to distinct legislative and Senate checks, with a separate dashed route to courts for legal disputes. The line geometry and landmark shapes carry the relationships; text labels clarify them.

The scene reuses Digest accents as wayfinding: blue for the ballot/result spine, equally weighted indigo and teal for the two measure outcomes, a gold marker at the later decision, and matching teal, indigo, and blue links between Governor powers and their checks. Softly tinted marker and gate interiors make the paths easier to scan without turning them into cards. The colors do not rate either ballot choice. Open and closed gates, branch endpoints, institution shapes, text labels, and a dashed legal-challenge route preserve the meaning without hue.

Tapping a landmark opens its rule and official source in a disclosure sheet. The full text version lists every relationship and source in order and can be linked directly with `view=text`. Narrow displays and enlarged text automatically show that version because tiny vector captions would be illegible; map controls are still exposed to accessibility as named buttons at ordinary display sizes. Stage jumps and disclosures have no animation, so reduced motion requires no alternative behavior. The existing [California guide](../apps/expo/src/components/ballot/CaliforniaGuidePreview.tsx) links the Governor and Proposition 4 examples. The [candidate detail](../apps/expo/src/app/contest-detail.tsx) offers the office map only when its district identifies California. The measure map appears from the 2026 official guide, whose API result is tied to that election date; the generic measure detail route does not have enough election identity to link safely. The scene grammar can be reused, but every new relationship requires a separately reviewed source record.

Federal and local roles need their own jurisdiction-specific source records. Federal executive power is checked by Congress and federal courts under federal law; local roles vary by charter and state law. Neither should inherit the California Governor's map through a title match.

## Reader validation before release

This is a design prototype. A moderated or unmoderated study should ask readers, without coaching: (1) What can the Governor do with a proposed budget? (2) What can the Governor not do alone? (3) Does a Yes vote on Proposition 4 create a financing program now? (4) What stays in place after a No result? (5) Where would you verify each claim? Record wrong answers and confusion about the path, then revise wording and retest. Screen-reader and large-text use should be included. This study has not yet been performed with readers, so the issue's user-testing criterion remains open.
