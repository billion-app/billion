# Content-detail design language

Every new Billion content type must follow this design language and the workflow below. The goal is a brief a reader can scan, explore, and verify: each stopping point should teach something true without requiring the whole article. Adapt the information architecture and interactions to the content type while sharing the app's typography, spacing, and source-disclosure conventions.

This is a product and implementation requirement. A new type is not complete when its source text has merely been wrapped in the bill layout or converted into a long Markdown explanation.

## Audience: zero assumed knowledge

Billion is for people who do not already follow politics. Assume no knowledge of a bill, its program, government procedure, or the subject itself. The opening must explain a recognizable idea and what an unfamiliar word means before asking the reader to explore. Naming “renewable energy subscriptions” or showing “51% of capacity” is not an explanation. Define an unavoidable term where it appears; essential meaning must not require a glossary tap. Do not invent a benefit, comparison, or savings estimate to make the topic feel personal.

Design follows that order: existing photo, familiar headline, short explanation of the basic idea, one consequence, one leading figure with its unit explained. Body text and the leading value carry attention; small gold context labels guide it. Remaining figures and research open on demand. An optional “On this page” menu provides the section jumps without placing three unfamiliar navigation decisions above the explanation. The change index is reached through the labeled layers action “What else would change?” after the current explanation, without a fraction counter at the top. Heat thresholds remain a labeled pair on their common scale, and complete date timelines keep their chronology; unrelated numbers do not compete as equal hero statistics.

## Shared language, specific structure

Use the editorial headings, readable body text, surface colors, fine dividers, spacing, and emphasis conventions exposed through [Expo styles](../apps/expo/src/styles.ts). Follow the [styling guide](expo-styling.md) rather than creating a separate palette. Color can help distinguish categories, but written labels and icons must carry their meaning; color must not imply that an outcome is good or bad.

Lead with an existing image when available and a short, standalone takeaway, then offer depth through flat editorial sections, reading modes, and disclosures. Do not add decorative card containers or placeholder artwork to fill space. Numeric facts sit directly on the navy canvas, grouped by typography and spacing. Do not add metric panels, rounded borders, filled containers or pill backgrounds. Choose those elements around the reader's questions and the source's actual structure. A legislative progress path makes sense for bills; it does not describe what a court has decided. Sharing the visual language does not require identical fields, section names, tabs, or interactions.

Prefer everyday words. When a legal or procedural term is necessary, define it inline where the reader encounters it, either in the sentence or through an accessible tap-to-define interaction. A glossary can supplement this but should not require a reader to leave the passage to understand it. Keep essential limits and unknowns visible alongside the explanation, rather than hiding qualifications behind optional detail.

## Explanation and evidence

Generated explanation, quoted source text, and outside commentary have different authority. Keep them visibly distinct:

- Identify Billion's AI explanation and make its provenance details available. A verified quote means the passage matched the source; it does not certify the surrounding analysis.
- Give claims a path to their supporting record. Use source disclosures for verified quotations, with a locator when available, and links to official documents. Do not fabricate a quote to fill a section.
- Preserve a separate original-text or record reading mode. The reader must be able to inspect the underlying text and open the official source independently of whether a brief exists.
- Attribute arguments and opinions to their speakers. Keep researched perspectives in the cited lens rather than presenting them as the official record. Omit unsupported detail and say what the record does not establish.

The [article-detail route](../apps/expo/src/app/article-detail.tsx) owns the shared reading modes, AI disclosure, and source-passage navigation. [Article generation](article-generation.md) explains grounding, quote checks, and the distinction between briefs and lenses.

## Current examples

Bills use the [bill schema](../packages/validators/src/bill-brief.ts) and [BillBrief](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/BillBrief.tsx) to explain a proposed or enacted change. The short summary expands into a fuller explanation; explicitly labeled comparisons separate the existing legal situation from a proposal or enacted change, affected-group disclosures explain concrete consequences, and unknowns preserve uncertainty. Key terms, cited history, and optional deeper reading support further exploration. Legal status comes from the source lifecycle, and the surrounding detail screen shows legislative progress only for bills.

### Rules for every bill interior

These rules apply through the shared bill components, including federal and state bills, proposed and enacted measures, and the overview and complete brief:

- Lead with the existing photo when available, the bill identity and explicit legal status, a standalone article headline that names its subject, a supplied summary, and the first concrete change. The explanation is visible without opening a disclosure. Missing photos leave a text opening.
- Use the existing navy, warm-white and restrained gold roles. Keep the body in flat sections; gold is the secondary color for brief legal-context and section cues, the record identifier, selected reading controls and small chart marks. Explanation text and prominent numeric values stay warm white; navigation, metric labels and evidence controls use the muted supporting role.
- Give numbers more prominence than labels. Use the existing warm white for prominent amounts, percentages, counts, durations and temperature values, with plain supporting labels; keep units, quantity prefixes such as “Within” and “Every”, denominators, scope and eligibility conditions adjacent. Long values and large text stack instead of shrinking or truncating. Dates and text-only facts remain complete readable values.
- Retain every supplied fact, including a single fact. Two or more distinct, complete calendar dates can form a chronological date list with small gold markers; relative deadlines and recurrence clauses remain ordinary facts. Comparing a change shows both supplied conditions on one flat vertical path, using a hollow current-state marker and a gold change marker. A numeric scale requires comparable quantities with an explicit common unit; unrelated amounts never become bars. The proposed heat-index graphic keeps its not-in-force qualifier visible and places additional supplied context behind its details disclosure.
- Keep one short supplied emphasis per paragraph. Secondary provisions, group effects, comparisons, quotations and deeper reading open on demand. Keep essential unknowns, legal status, and the distinction between generated explanation and original text visible.
- Use the shared short reveals and press feedback, respect Reduce Motion and Dynamic Type, and retain source links. Check representative money, percentage, duration, date and no-number bills rather than tailoring the screen to one record.

[BillOverview](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/BillOverview.tsx) and [BillBrief](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/BillBrief.tsx) use the same [fact renderer](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/BillFactFigure.tsx) and [fact selection/formatting rules](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/bill-fact-figure.ts). Bills without structured analysis retain their existing Markdown/original-text fallback; the renderer does not invent missing summaries or facts.

Every structured bill has a visual, including briefs with no numeric facts. The shared [visual selector](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/bill-visual.ts) prefers a supported heat-threshold scale, complete-date timeline, or explicit percentage plot. Other briefs show a [change map](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/BillChangeMap.tsx): parallel branches connect the supplied provisions, with small semantic glyphs and complete titles. A branch reveals its existing explanation, comparison and source passage. Branches replace the secondary-provision list in the overview, rather than adding another copy of that list. They describe relationships, not chronology, impact scores or a claim that the brief covers every provision. The complete brief uses the same consequence explorer for numeric and nonnumeric bills; the overview retains this map for bills without a supported numeric visual.

Percentage plots use an explicitly labeled 0–100% axis and preserve the supplied label, value, qualifiers and note. They do not label a number as a share or turn it into a pie chart. Out-of-range, ranged and compound quantities remain written facts and receive the change-map fallback when no other supported chart is available. All supplied facts remain accessible. Large text stacks the metric grid and retains the visual; stacked temperature figures add labels below the scale so each marker remains identifiable. Gold is limited to the map origin, selected branch glyph and small chart marks; titles and amounts stay warm white on navy. No image generation or content regeneration is involved.

### Interactive exploration

The overview keeps its photo, supplied summary and first consequence visible. Its attribution and optional “On this page” control share one quiet row. The menu opens a native sheet with three plain questions: What would change? Who could this affect? What is still unknown? Existing subtle icons identify these destinations inside the sheet. Choosing one dismisses the sheet and scrolls to its measured section position; absent sections have no destination. Positions update after disclosures change the page height, and Reduce Motion disables animated scrolling.

[BillChangeExplorer](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/BillChangeExplorer.tsx) is used by every structured full brief and by overviews with fact visuals. It shows one complete consequence, with comparison and evidence available on demand. The full brief includes the selected supplied headline; the overview omits the first provision title because the explanation follows its article opening. The article headline has its own field; it must identify the program or action without relying on the summary. It replaces the full brief's repeated legal-kind labels and accordion stack, so the reader always sees an explanation rather than a closed list. A single supplied change has no unnecessary navigation.

A quiet legal-context label introduces the explanation. The visible text says “If it becomes law”, with enacted records retaining “Under this law”. After the explanation, “What else would change?” opens the existing native index of complete supplied headlines. Choosing a provision closes the sheet and replaces the explanation, resetting its supporting disclosures. The selected position is conveyed in accessible text and in the sheet rather than a fraction counter in the reading flow. The index and section-menu buttons retain 44-point touch heights and the sheets scroll at enlarged text sizes. The overview's nonnumeric change map keeps its existing branches.

In the shared fact renderer, a number, percentage plot, temperature marker or dated milestone can select one inline explanation. It shows the supplied value, note, available current-law context and a source-passage action. Selecting another fact replaces the explanation; Close clears it. Changing the expanded fact list or opening the complete details also clears selection. The overview scrolls the selected explanation into view using measured offsets. Values without supporting context remain static, rather than accepting a tap with no result. Existing numeric limits stay visible before interaction. Figures are selected, never edited: no slider changes a legal threshold or invents an outcome. Full-brief figures reuse the same selection behavior.

The exploration UI stays on navy without cards, fills or pill controls. Gold is confined to the selected index marker, selected figure underline and existing chart marks. Reveals and press feedback reuse the reader motion helpers. A disclosure chevron marks explorable facts instead of repeating an instruction beneath every figure heading. The Source view keeps document locators beside the exact quotation. UI strings are available in English, Spanish and Chinese. No new telemetry, remote experiment or content-generation call is introduced.

### Consequence before mechanism

A change with a supplied `reader` introduction starts with its short everyday consequence and a useful question. The question has a help icon and opens a compact floating window, preserving the article's scroll position. Answer and Source share that window, with an explicit Close action and outside-tap dismissal. The question window has only Answer and Source; it does not offer a third Compare mode. Cached explanations and comparison presentations retain the inline comparison of both legal conditions. The window reuses the existing navy canvas, warm-white text and inactive action labels, with gold reserved for the selected action, fades in unless Reduce Motion is enabled, and scrolls within the safe area at enlarged text sizes. Labeled icon actions select each view without nested disclosures. The comparison retains the complete current-law and proposed/enacted conditions, and Source retains the exact quotation, locator and route to the full document. A source without a quotation has no invented Source action. Older cached explanations offer Compare and Source directly, without an extra Details & sources gate. These controls do not repeat downward chevrons or nest evidence accordions. The source callback and original wording stay intact.

Affected-group rows open a native detail sheet with the complete supplied effect rather than stretching the article into another accordion. The reading links at the end open the story, full brief and metadata directly; there is no Go deeper gate. More open questions keeps its useful single list reveal with a help/minus action. Icons retain text labels and 44-point touch targets; icons alone are not the explanation.

The figure footer has one labeled activity action. All numbers & sources opens the remaining figures and supporting context together, including all supplied source links; Close details closes them. Temperature and date views retain their contextual labels. Targeted metric inspection remains available. This combines the previous More figures and Details & sources controls rather than adding another nested layer.

The generation and translation contract is described in [article generation](article-generation.md#short-introductions-to-individual-changes). Local draft copy is review evidence, not a published replacement for a stored brief.

### Supporting information has supporting weight

The explanation and its supplied key phrase lead the reading flow; numeric values lead the supporting figure. Quick section links, legal-context labels, metric labels and evidence disclosures use the existing readable 15/22 supporting role. They retain 44-point touch targets. Pure numeric groups omit the generic “In numbers” label; temperature, date and text-only headings retain their semantic context in the supporting role. Figure boundaries and the rule above the consequence are omitted so whitespace groups related information without turning every block into a separate destination. All figure notes, qualifiers and original source access remain available. The color hierarchy is explicit: warm-white explanation and prominent values first; small gold legal-context and section cues second; muted navigation, metric labels and evidence controls third. Gold cues retain the 15/22 supporting size and do not use extra bold weight. Generic headings remain omitted, and explanatory notes retain their ordinary text color.

The reader's application of Karri Saarinen's [Output isn’t design](https://linear.app/now/output-isn-t-design), [Every feature should earn its place](https://karrisaarinen.com/posts/every-feature-should-earn-its-place/) and the team's [Linear redesign account](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui) is to evaluate the entire reading view: every label, icon and disclosure needs a reader-facing purpose. These sources inform our judgment; they do not prescribe a Billion layout or palette.

A text-only fact is supporting information, rather than a statistic deserving a large callout. Key details uses a small gold heading, muted 15/22 supplied labels and 17/26 values, with 4-point internal gaps and 16-point gaps between facts. These facts omit the generic time/energy glyph; the disclosure chevron still identifies an action. The original wording and visible essential notes remain intact. Actual numbers retain their existing prominent treatment and complete units. Calendar timelines and temperature scales keep their existing visual structure.

Supporting full-brief section headings use the same sans-serif action role as Key details; serif remains for story headlines. The full brief's Who it affects rows show complete group names and disclosure chevrons. Direction labels appear in sentence case only after opening a group, next to the supplied explanation. They are written as potential benefits, potential drawbacks, mixed effects or effects uncertain, without colored outcomes. This removes unexplained status labels from the scan path while preserving uncertainty in the expanded explanation and the visible unknowns section. The full-brief expansion shows the complete supplied effect once, without repeating its short takeaway first. One group opens at a time using the reader's existing motion behavior. No extra navigation or content-generation step is introduced.

### Measured reader geometry

Numeric figures can include a compact “Today:” comparison (“Before this law:” for enacted bills). The renderer reuses a complete supplied `before` statement only when the fact and exactly one change cite the same nonempty passage in the same document. It does not infer a baseline, calculate an increase, or equate a missing legal minimum with zero actual participation. Statements over 160 characters remain in the full comparison without being truncated. The comparison has a warm-white label and muted copy inside Details & sources in both overview and full brief.

The default numeric section shows one leading metric at full width, preferring an explicitly supported percentage when supplied. All numbers & sources reveals the remaining values, including the second value of a two-fact record. Expanded numeric figures retain the complete supplied list and original order in a single vertical reading path. They do not return to a grid of unrelated values. The same action opens supporting comparison paragraphs, prose and citations. Explicit numeric limits and conditional notes stay beside their values; units and quantity prefixes are never hidden. These controls reuse the existing short disclosure animations. Numeric metrics have no enclosing boundary, fill, corner radius or internal panel padding. Their existing supplied labels use regular 15/22 supporting text, without leading symbols or repeated disclosure chevrons; no replacement policy category or emoji is invented. Columns have a 24-point gutter and the page inset remains 24 points. Chart geometry uses the full measured column width. Label measurement still aligns paired numeric values. Calendar lists, text-only facts and the shared temperature figure retain their open layout. The percentage track uses a four-point rounded stroke, with its exact gold marker and labeled endpoints. A supplied percentage note of at most 80 characters uses complete supporting text directly on the canvas, without a tint or tag container; longer notes remain ordinary text. Gold does not imply enactment or a good/bad outcome. Numeric values remain tappable when source context exists, while the explicit Details & sources disclosure also provides access to the supporting information. Date and text-only fact disclosures retain their chevrons.

The reader follows [Apple’s typography guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography): use comfortably readable sizes and weights, retain Dynamic Type, and adapt layout instead of shrinking text. The 17-point reading and action sizes and 15-point secondary size are Billion’s chosen hierarchy; Apple’s minimum is not a target for routine copy. [Progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) keeps optional evidence available without surrounding the primary explanation with repeated captions. Essential legal qualifiers and units stay visible.

The approved visual hierarchy stays fixed while the layout adapts to its actual content:

- Page inset and fact-column gutters are 24 points. Internal groups use 8/16/24-point spacing. Reader type uses display 30/36, title 20/28, body 17/26, secondary labels 15/22 and actions 17/24. Reading text and interaction labels are not reduced to caption sizes to fit more on screen. Numeric callouts remain 40/48 and temperature values 48/56.
- The fact grid measures its own width. In physical pixels, available width equals the sum of the column widths and gutter. Rounding remainders are distributed so columns differ by at most one physical pixel. Actual measured label heights align paired values; a quantity prefix reserves the same line in both columns. A value that wraps triggers a stacked layout instead of shrinking or clipping.
- Date/comparison markers are centered at half the first text line's scaled height, rounded to the device pixel grid. One shared [timeline row](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/ReaderTimelineRow.tsx) extends the connector to the next marker, including wrapped content and the inter-row gap. Date rows are chronological steps, not a quantitative elapsed-time scale.
- Numeric chart positions use `x = width × (value − minimum) / (maximum − minimum)`. The SVG viewBox matches the measured width so circles stay round. Invalid or zero-width domains suppress the chart; the written values remain. [Geometry tests](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/reader-geometry.test.ts) verify physical-pixel totals and numeric scale positions.
- Solid palette contrast against `#0E1530` is 16.37:1 for warm white, 8.55:1 for gold, and 5.58:1 for metadata. These values use the sRGB relative-luminance formula `(lighter + 0.05) / (darker + 0.05)`; they do not certify text over photos, translucent states, or an accessibility audit of the entire app. Existing 44-point disclosure targets and system Reduce Motion remain in place.

Court cases use a separate [court schema](../packages/validators/src/court-brief.ts) and [CourtBrief](../apps/expo/src/components/ui/CourtBrief.tsx). The reading starts with what the court did and what request it was deciding. Reasoning, separate opinions, ordered versus possible effects, and unresolved questions each retain their own meaning. Emergency relief is not a final decision on every underlying legal question. The UI offers inline legal-term definitions, an Opinions mode when opinions are available, and a Court record mode. Individual points link to official documents, and source disclosures lead into the original text. Court, docket, date, and proceeding identify the record without inventing a legislative timeline.

These are examples of adapting the same language, not templates whose fields must be copied into the next type.

## Required workflow for a new content type

1. Read representative official records, including sparse and ambiguous examples. Identify the reader's questions, the type's legal or procedural states, and the distinctions that a summary must preserve.
2. Design the reading structure and interactions around those questions. Decide what belongs in the short takeaway, what needs its own section or mode, where terms need definitions, and how a reader reaches evidence from each section.
3. Define a versioned structured-output schema in `@acme/validators` for those concepts. Separate generated fields from source-derived metadata; represent attribution, uncertainty, and source references explicitly. Reuse shared primitives where they fit without forcing the type into bill or court semantics.
4. Connect generation, deterministic verification, persistence, and the API to that schema. Keep original records independent and preserve a usable fallback for missing, invalid, or stale briefs. Follow the [architecture](architecture.md) and [generation guide](article-generation.md) for package boundaries and provenance.
5. Build the renderer using the shared visual language, then verify the actual detail screen with representative records. Check scanning, long text, missing sections, definitions, reading-mode transitions, quote disclosures, passage navigation, and official links. Confirm that the UI preserves legal status and attribution and never presents generated prose as source text. Use the applicable checks in [Contributing](../CONTRIBUTING.md#check-your-change).

## Next adaptation: executive orders

Executive orders are the next content type that still needs to be adapted to structured output. They currently use Markdown explainers; a dedicated structured executive-order brief and its content-specific detail experience are not implemented. Apply the workflow above to their directives, authority, affected agencies or people, timing, and limits, grounded in representative records. Treat that as upcoming design work, not as an existing schema or screen contract.
