# Proposition consequence analysis

The California proposition detail route leads with the official title, then a reviewed Billion explanation when one is available. The explanation separates the rule today, Yes, No, implementation, affected groups, costs and funding, and conditions and unknowns. Stacked outcome cards provide the same text to visual and screen-reader users; they do not imply that either outcome is desirable. The official summary, fiscal analysis, voting outcomes, submitted arguments and proposed-law link remain independent.

The [versioned schema](../packages/validators/src/proposition-consequences.ts) gives every explanatory claim source identifiers. The [server gate](../packages/api/src/lib/proposition-consequences.ts) requires an approved revision, matching review revision/hash, exact election/number/title/URL, available official summary/voting outcomes/fiscal analysis, valid unique citations, and a captured guide snapshot matching the current guide. Changes to any hashed guide field hide the explanation. Retrieval refreshes alone do not invalidate unchanged content. Evidence snapshots remain server-side; Expo receives analysis, source links and retrieval dates through tRPC.

The bounded store is a committed revision registry, initially empty. No model runs or database writes occur during guide reads. Publishing or rolling back is an explicit reviewed code change: put the approved immutable revision first in `reviewedPropositionRevisions`. Reordering can restore a prior approved revision only while its source hash still matches. Never edit the content of an approved revision; create a new revision and review it.

## Editorial and delivery gates

This is a gated implementation for #426 and the publication boundary of #409, not a claim that either issue's editorial criteria are complete. No approved analysis is included. #334 still requires an editor to assess representative measures, citations, factual accuracy, unequal evidence and Yes/No treatment. Approval must record reviewer, time, exact revision/hash and findings. Automated shape/citation/hash checks do not prove that a claim follows from a passage or that a conditional fiscal effect is accurately explained.

Before populating the registry, capture official guide inputs plus LAO analysis and proposed-law text, with retrieval dates. An editor must inspect every claim beside those snapshots, confirm amounts/dates and conditional language, and document unknown fiscal effects. Supplemental snapshots do not refresh automatically: a new source capture or detected change requires a new revision and renewed review. The gate detects changes in the guide record, not upstream content that ingestion has not refreshed.

Remaining #409 work is an explicit bounded generator, prompt/model version capture from that generator, supplemental source refresh/invalidation, deterministic amount/date checks, and a durable editor review interface. The read-only review tool exposes captured source identity and tests the current gate; it does not generate, approve or persist content. Use the [scraper operations guide](../apps/scraper/README.md#proposition-review-dry-run) for its commands.

Before reader-facing completion, approve two substantively different real measures (including conditional succession and unknown net fiscal effects), validate readers' understanding of both outcomes, and verify the production native flow. Synthetic fixtures exercise rendering and rejection behavior but do not satisfy those gates. #403 / PR #405 governance visuals remain separate; this renderer does not embed that prototype or imply its sources are approved.

The reading hierarchy puts the headline, current rule and paired voting outcomes first. The full official title remains beside the labeled Billion headline through an explicit disclosure; review metadata and deduplicated source details are under “Sources and review”, with each source mapped to the claims it supports. Implementation opens separately. Affected people, costs and material uncertainty remain visible. Official summaries and fiscal analysis have their own disclosures; empty sections are omitted. An unavailable explanation offers the official guide immediately rather than repeating missing-field notices.

An optional reviewed `decisionNote` puts a material qualifier, such as who repays authorized borrowing, inside the voting comparison. It is a cited explanatory claim subject to the same publication gate, never inferred from a title or created by the client. Adding it to an approved draft requires a new immutable revision and editorial review.

An optional `decisionMap` supplies three concise cited claims (`today`, `yes`, `no`, each at most 160 characters). The UI shows the present rule leading to two equally styled alternatives, with the full voting explanation available beneath it. Each node must preserve material conditions; it is reviewed evidence, not a client-generated summary. All three nodes are required together and pass the same citation gate. Older revisions without the map retain the full prose comparison. Material qualifiers and limits remain visible.

Source disclosures retain the complete text of each cited claim under its supporting source. Section names alone are insufficient when two statements in one section use different evidence. The capture fixtures exercise that case without restoring repeated citation links to the primary reading flow.
